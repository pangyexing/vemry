# Start Here

This is one learner's vault, the week of 24 to 30 September 2026. They are reading *Attention Is All You Need* a chapter at a time, rebuilding its attention block before presenting it at Friday's group meeting, keeping what they learn on cards, and following one sceptical thread: whether attention weights explain anything. Every file is plain Markdown; open the folder in any editor and it is all there.

The dates read from Wednesday 30 September.

## Try this

1. Open [[2026-09-30]], go to the empty line at the end and type `/`. Type `call` and press Enter, then `qu` and Enter: a question callout
2. Press ⌘P (Ctrl+P on Windows and Linux) and type `translate`. Every command in the app is in that list, this vault's two scripts included
3. Press ⌥⇧Space (Alt+Shift+Space) in any app, type a line and press Enter: it lands at the end of [[inbox]]. With an AI set up, pick *File the inbox* and approve where each capture goes
4. Ask the AI to list every note in this vault. `private/` is not in the answer, and nothing in the answer says a folder is missing
5. Open [[遗忘曲线与间隔重复]], a handout a lab mate shared in Chinese, and press ⌘⇧T: each paragraph's translation sits beside it. [[papers/vaswani-2017/source]] translates the other way
6. Open the source link of [[Ebbinghaus 1885, Chapter 7]] in the built-in browser, select a sentence and press *Selection → note*
7. Open [[01 Scaled dot-product attention]] and press Run on the experiment: drag $d_k$ with the scale off and watch the weights collapse onto one key. Then ask the AI to check the retelling under *In my words*
8. Open the Review view: five cards are due, two of them left by that check
9. Open the Board and tick a task: the line in [[Reproducing the Transformer]] changes with it
10. Open Session Replay: a retelling checked and turned into cards, an edit refused at the gate that never reached the file, and the inbox being filed
