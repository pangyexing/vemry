# Walker 2017

Notes on the selective-deprivation chapter. The design is the interesting part: wake people during one stage and leave the other intact, then test recall.

> [!evidence] Suppressing slow-wave sleep hurts recall of word pairs; suppressing REM does not.
> supports:[[Sleep and Memory#^sws]]
> The asymmetry is what makes it evidence for a stage-specific mechanism rather than for "sleep is restorative in general". ^walker-sws

> [!evidence] The effect is dose-dependent — more slow-wave sleep, better recall
> supports:[[Sleep and Memory#^consolidation]]

Caveat worth keeping in view: waking someone repeatedly is not a clean intervention. Filed against [[Open Questions]].
