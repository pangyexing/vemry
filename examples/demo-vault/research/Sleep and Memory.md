# Sleep and Memory

The thread I keep coming back to: what sleep actually does to something I learned
during the day. Sources live in [[Walker 2017]] and [[Rasch 2013]] — one note each, pointing
back at the claims below.

## Claims

> [!claim] Sleep after learning consolidates declarative memory rather than merely preventing interference
> The passive account says sleep helps because nothing new arrives to overwrite the
> trace. The active account says consolidation is work the sleeping brain does. ^consolidation

> [!evidence] Rasch & Born, 2013 — a review of consolidation during sleep
> supports:[[Sleep and Memory#^consolidation]]
> Full note: [[Rasch 2013]]

> [!claim] Slow-wave sleep matters more for facts than REM does ^sws

> [!evidence] Walker, 2017 — selective deprivation separates the two stages
> supports:[[Sleep and Memory#^sws]]
> Full note: [[Walker 2017]]

> [!claim] A short nap recovers part of the same effect ^nap

That last one has no evidence attached yet — it shows up in the Evidence view as a
claim nothing supports, which is exactly the point of that view.
