# Sleep and Memory

The thread I keep coming back to: what sleep actually does to something I learned during the day. The sources are one note each in `research/evidence/`, pointing back at the claims below.

## Claims

> [!claim] Sleep after learning consolidates declarative memory rather than merely preventing interference.
> The passive account says sleep helps because nothing new arrives to overwrite the trace. The active account says consolidation is work the sleeping brain does. ^consolidation

> [!evidence] Rasch & Born, 2013 — a review of consolidation during sleep ([[Rasch 2013]])
> supports:[[Sleep and Memory#^consolidation]]

> [!claim] Slow-wave sleep matters more for facts than REM does ^sws

> [!evidence] Walker, 2017 — selective deprivation separates the two stages ([[Walker 2017]])
> supports:[[Sleep and Memory#^sws]]

> [!claim] A short nap recovers part of the same effect ^nap

The nap claim has nothing behind it yet. That is what [[Nap Experiment]] is for — and [[Open Questions]] says how much it can show.

## Why it matters here

The cards in [[cards/Sleep]] and [[papers/vaswani-2017/cards]] are what the experiment measures: an evening's grades are a recall score I was going to produce anyway.
